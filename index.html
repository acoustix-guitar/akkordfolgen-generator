Quick start

  1. Choose style, length, mood, key and harmonic complexity in the Generator panel.
  2. Press “Generate Progression”. The chords appear as bars. “Generate Another” gives you a new idea, “Reset” returns to the defaults.
  3. Press “Play” in the bar at the bottom. The progression repeats until you press “Stop”.
  4. Open the Mixer with the “Mixer” button. Each part has a switch (Arp, Chords, Strums, Bass, Metro, Drums) and a volume fader.
  5. The buttons Player, Arp, Chords, Strums, Bass and Drums open the settings of each part above the bottom bar.
  6. Save your work with “Save progression”, or export sound with “Export MIDI file” or “Export WAV file”.

The bottom bar

  • Play and Stop start and stop the playback.
  • Repeat plays the progression again and again. Count-in plays leading bars first; their number is set in the Player panel.
  • Tempo: use the slider or the buttons − and + (one beat per minute per click).
  • The four circles show the current beat.
  • Mixer: every part has its own switch and its own fader. “Master” sets the overall volume. “Metro” is the metronome.
  • All parts are independent: block chords, strums, arpeggio, bass and drums can be switched on alone or in any combination.

Chords and progression

  • Style, length, mood, key and harmonic complexity steer the generator. “Simple” uses basic chords, “Advanced” also uses richer chords.
  • Click a bar to edit its chords. Type up to four chords separated by spaces, for example C Am F G, and press Enter.
  • In the edit field: Escape cancels, an empty field deletes the bar, and a vertical bar “|” splits the text into two bars.
  • “Add bar” and “Remove last bar” change the length of the progression.
  • “Show Roman numerals” shows the position of each chord in the key (I, IV, V …).
  • Chord notation in the header: International (B, B♭) or German (H, B). Language switches the whole page between English and German.

Block chords

Block chords play all notes of a chord at the same time. Switch them on with “Chords” in the Mixer; their settings are in the panel “Chords”.

  • Sound: piano, organ, pad, strings, marimba, steel or nylon guitar. A SoundFont can be loaded as well.
  • Octave: the buttons − and + move the block chords up or down by whole octaves. This does not change any other part.
  • Without the option “Play block chords in this rhythm” each chord is played once per chord change. With it, the chord is struck in the pattern of the matrix.
  • Matrix: click a cell to switch it on, then soft, then off. Each column is one sixteenth note.
  • “Spread the notes slightly like a strum” is only for the sound steel or nylon guitar. It is off by default, so block chords stay real block chords.

Guitar strumming

Guitar strumming is a separate layer with its own panel, matrix, switch (“Strums” in the Mixer), volume, octave and effects. It does not depend on the block chords.

  • Every hit is played string by string in standard tuning (E A D G B e). Common chords use the real guitar shapes, other chords use movable barre shapes or a calculated shape.
  • Matrix, top row: click a cell to switch it on (filled dot), then soft (empty dot), then chuck (×), then off.
  • A chuck is a muted stroke: the fretting hand dampens the strings, and you hear a short percussive “chk”.
  • Matrix, second row: the arrows show down-strokes (↓) and up-strokes (↑). Click an arrow to flip the direction.
  • Patterns: choose a ready-made guitar rhythm, for example folk, pop, boom-chuck, or draw your own.
  • Settings: strum speed, humanize, down-stroke and up-stroke level, how many strings up-strokes play, pick attack (soft to hard) and ring-out (muted to ringing).
  • Sound: built-in steel or nylon guitar, or a SoundFont. The built-in sound has a guitar body and a short pick noise at the start of every stroke.
  • You can save a strumming pattern as a file (JSON with all settings, or a text file with rhythm and strokes) and load it again.

Arpeggiator and guitar tablature

The arpeggiator plays the notes of each chord one after the other (fingerpicking). Switch it on with “Arp” in the Mixer.

  • Matrix: each row is a tone of the chord (bass, root, 3rd, 5th, …), each column is one sixteenth note. Click a cell to switch it on, then soft, then off. Several rows in one column play together.
  • Pattern: choose a ready-made picking pattern or draw your own. Note length: one step, or ringing until the next hit.
  • “Play the notes of the guitar chord shape” makes the rows the strings of the real guitar chord. Then you hear exactly what the tablature shows. This option is on by default.
  • Octave: the buttons − and + move the arpeggio by whole octaves.
  • Tablature: six lines for the strings e, B, G, D, A, E (top to bottom). The numbers are fret numbers; 0 is an open string. Soft notes are shown in grey.
  • “Chord shown” chooses the chord. “Follows the playing chord” changes the display while the music plays. In a bar with two chords only the steps of the shown chord are filled.
  • The chord diagram next to the tablature shows the grip of the shown chord: × = string not played, ○ = open string, dots = fingers. For grips higher up the neck, the number of the first fret is written beside the diagram.
  • “Export tablature as PNG” saves a picture of the tablature of the whole progression, four bars per line. You can give the file a name.

Bass sequencer

The bass plays root, third, fifth, sixth, seventh or octave of each chord in the low register. Switch it on with “Bass” in the Mixer.

  • Rhythm: by default the bass follows the kick drum. Choose “Own pattern” or edit any cell to draw your own bass rhythm.
  • Sound: fingered, picked, acoustic or synth bass, or a SoundFont.
  • Octave: one octave down to two octaves up. The bass is already very low, so one octave down can be hard to hear on small speakers.

Drums and rhythm editor

  • Choose the sound set (drum kit or cajon), the genre, a ready-made rhythm and the time signature (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/8).
  • Pattern editor: click a cell to switch it on, then soft, then off. Each row has a round volume knob.
  • Swing delays the off-beat notes for a shuffled feel.
  • Own WAV samples can replace the built-in drum sounds.
  • “Export drum groove” saves only the drums as a MIDI or WAV file.

Reverb and chorus

  • Every part has round knobs under “Effects” at the top of its panel: Reverb (room sound) and Chorus (a wider, shimmering sound).
  • The drums have reverb only, no chorus.
  • 0 % means the effect is off. Use a little: 10 to 30 % is often enough.
  • Turn a knob by dragging up or down, with the mouse wheel, or with the keyboard: arrow keys change by 5, Page Up and Page Down by 10, Home sets 0 %, End sets 100 %. A double click or the Delete key resets it to 0 %.
  • The effects are part of the WAV export, but not of the MIDI export. The settings are kept only while the page stays open.

Sounds and SoundFonts

  • Block chords, arpeggio, bass and strums can each load their own SoundFont (a file ending in .sf2). Load the file, choose a preset, then choose “SoundFont” in the sound list of that part.
  • Only plain .sf2 files work, not .sf3 or .sfz. The file stays on your computer and must be loaded again after every visit.
  • Large SoundFonts need a lot of memory in the browser. Small and medium files are safest.
  • Own samples (WAV files named by note, for example piano_C4.wav) can be loaded in the Player panel.

Save, load and export

  • Save progression: saves the chords with key, time signature and tempo as a small file (JSON). A plain text file with chords such as C Am F G, one bar per line, can be loaded too. You can type a file name.
  • Preset packs: a pack is a file with your own presets. A preset can hold a progression plus arpeggio, bass, block chord rhythm and guitar strumming. You can create packs, add your current setup, save packs to a file and load packs from your computer.
  • Export MIDI file: a standard MIDI file with separate tracks for chords, arpeggio, bass, strums and drums, for example for a DAW or notation program.
  • Export WAV file: an audio file (16 bit) with the sound of the app, including effects. Rendering takes a few seconds.
  • The tick boxes in the export area decide what is included: repeats, drums, count-in, block chords, arpeggio and bass.

Keyboard and accessibility

  • Everything can be used with the keyboard: Tab moves forward, Shift+Tab backward, Enter or Space presses a button or switches a cell.
  • In the matrices the arrow keys move from cell to cell.
  • Escape closes an open panel and returns to its button.
  • Controls have text labels for screen readers. The design is bright with strong contrast, and the panels fold up from the bottom bar so that the progression stays in view.

If something does not work

  • No sound: check that the switch of the part is on in the Mixer, that its fader is not at 0 %, and that the device volume is up. Browsers only start audio after a click, so press Play once.
  • The message “Nothing to play”: switch on at least one part in the Mixer.
  • German or English does not switch: the language files need the page to be opened from a web address (http or https). If the page is opened as a plain file from the computer, only English is available.
  • A SoundFont does not load or sounds wrong: use a plain .sf2 file of moderate size and choose “SoundFont” in the sound list of the part.
  • The page remembers your language and chord notation. Preset packs stay in this browser until you remove them. Everything else is lost when the page is closed; save your progression to a file to keep it.
